## Why the headline price tells you almost nothing

Two ERP vendors can quote the same annual figure and deliver very different five-year costs. The difference is the licensing model. A per-user quote grows with headcount. A consumption quote grows with transaction volume or computing resources. An unlimited-user quote is fixed but usually carries a higher platform fee. Before you compare numbers, you must know which model sits behind each number.

Ask every vendor for the same three things: the licensing metric, the unit of that metric, and the annual uplift clause. A quote that does not state all three is not comparable.

## The five licensing models you will meet

### Named user

Each person who logs in needs a licence. Cost rises one-for-one with the number of accounts. This model is predictable for small teams and expensive for organisations that want wide access. It also punishes occasional users, so buyers often under-license and share credentials, which breaks audit trails.

### Concurrent user

The vendor counts sessions active at the same time, not accounts. A company with three shifts can license fewer concurrent sessions than named users. The risk is queueing at shift change and month-end close, when many people log in together. Model the peak hour, not the average day.

### Consumption or resource-based

The vendor charges for modules, storage, transactions or computing resources. Acumatica is the best-known example in the mid-market, and its own comparison pages describe resource consumption as the pricing basis. This model rewards wide adoption because adding a user costs little, but the bill can move when volume grows. Ask for the metered units and the price per unit in writing.

### Module or edition tiers

The vendor sells a base platform plus optional modules such as manufacturing, warehouse management, project accounting or field service. Each module adds a line item. Compare the module list against your requirement list, because a lower base price often hides three modules you need.

### Unlimited user

The vendor charges a platform fee and does not meter users. This is attractive for manufacturers and retailers with large shop-floor or store populations. Check what "unlimited" excludes: often it covers named employees but not external partners, customer portals or API integrations.

## Count the users vendors forget to quote

Most cost overruns come from accounts that were not in the first count. Build the count from these groups:

- Finance, purchasing, sales and operations staff who log in weekly.
- Shop-floor or warehouse staff who scan, book time or confirm shipments.
- Approvers who only open the system to release a purchase order or expense.
- Read-only users such as auditors, controllers and department heads.
- Integration accounts for e-commerce, CRM, payroll, EDI or banking.
- Sandbox and test environments, which many vendors licence separately.
- Implementation partner consultants during the project.
- Former employees whose accounts stay active after departure.

Write the count per group, then ask each vendor to price that exact list. If a vendor cannot price a group, treat the gap as risk, not as a saving.

## Build a five-year comparison

Use the same user count, the same module list and the same contract length for every vendor. Put these rows in one spreadsheet:

1. Year-one subscription at the quoted user count.
2. Year-two to year-five subscription, including the contractual uplift.
3. Implementation services, which are usually quoted separately from subscription.
4. Data migration, integration and customisation effort.
5. Training and change management.
6. Sandbox, storage and API overage charges.
7. Exit or data-extraction fees stated in the contract.

Add the rows, then divide by the number of users you will actually deploy. The result is cost per user per year, and it is the only figure that compares fairly across licensing models.

## Questions to put in the request for proposal

- Which metric do you meter, and how is it measured?
- What happens when we exceed the licensed count for one month?
- Are read-only, approver and integration accounts licensed?
- Is a non-production environment included?
- What is the annual uplift, and is it capped?
- What is the price if we reduce users at renewal?
- Are modules priced per user or per site?
- What are the exit and data-export fees?

Get the answers in the contract, not in the proposal deck. A pricing model that is not written into the agreement can change at the next renewal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is per-user pricing cheaper than consumption pricing?

It depends on user count and volume. Per-user pricing is cheaper when few people need access and volume is high. Consumption pricing is cheaper when many people need access and volume is modest. Model both at your real numbers before deciding.

### Should we license every employee?

License the people who do work in the system. Give occasional viewers a read-only licence or a report, not a full seat. Do not share credentials to avoid licence cost, because shared accounts destroy the audit trail you need for financial controls.

### Does the implementation partner need licences?

Usually yes, at least during the project. Ask whether partner access is included, priced per consultant or covered by a temporary project licence. This line often appears late in negotiation.
